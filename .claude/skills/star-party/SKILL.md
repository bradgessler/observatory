# Star party

Learned on 8 October 2026: a crooked tripod aligned by camera, the camera swapped for an eyepiece,
a driveway full of 10 to 12 year olds, and Saturn to finish. The box holds each target; Brad hands
the eyepiece round; the session picks targets, sends Go Tos and supplies the facts.

## Before anyone looks

1. **Tracking proven before the camera comes off**: two plate solves a minute apart, drift under
   0.5"/s (8 Oct: 0.19"/s, an hour in a 25 mm eyepiece). Say the number. Then, and only then, tell
   Brad: camera off (its switch first, then the USB), diagonal and widest eyepiece in, clutches
   untouched. One instruction at a time; never flash while people are at the eyepiece.
2. **Counterweight side told**, not guessed (`drive-the-box`). Dry-run the whole tour from the pose
   the mount is in (`Pointing.landing/4`): which targets are a small move on this side and which
   need the big swing over the meridian. Put the swing last, with someone watching the cables.
3. **Square "up" on the pad**: press Up on the D-pad; Center page Backwards / Swapped until it moves
   the right way; a diagonal-ish move means turn the diagonal. A meridian flip turns the view 180
   degrees: both Backwards keys after the swing.
4. **Pad speed**: kids hunt with the ball at full tilt (800x, 3 deg/s, no soft limits on a mount
   never homed, and full-tilt ramps trip the driver's stall watch, which ends the hold). Tell Brad
   the D-pad is for small moves; if the hold ends, send the Go To again rather than hunting.

## The tour: build up, end on the best

Faint smudges first, colour and planets last. "Start with the lamest stuff, amp it up, end on
Saturn." Nebulae are smudges by eye; a gold-and-blue double or Saturn's rings is the wow.

| Order | Kind | Examples (season permitting) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a nebula, as a "ghost" | Dumbbell, Ring |
| 2 | a puzzle | Double Double (two stars, each splits in two at high power) |
| 3 | colour | Albireo (gold and blue), Gamma Delphini |
| 4 | a galaxy core | Andromeda ("another galaxy, a trillion stars") |
| 5 | the finale | Saturn, Jupiter, the Moon |

Check altitudes against the trees before promising anything (`Tonight`), and say when the finale
clears them ("Saturn's over the tree at about 9:30").

## Facts that landed

Plain, physical, with a "when did the light leave" and an "our Sun will" where true. Give
temperature, size and distance when asked (8 Oct: "give me the temperature, size, and distance").

- **Albireo**: gold giant ~4,000 C, ~70x the Sun across; blue ~13,000 C, ~3x, spins so fast it's
  flattened; ~400-430 ly, light left around 1600 (Galileo); Gaia says they may not be a pair.
- **Ring Nebula**: not an explosion. A Sun-like star puffed its layers off ~4,000 years ago; light
  left ~2,500 years ago (ancient Greece); a barrel seen end-on; our Sun will make one in ~5 billion
  years; white dwarf the size of Earth at 100,000+ C.
- **Double Double**: four stars ~160 ly; pairs orbit in ~700 and ~1,800 years, the two pairs in
  ~half a million (rough: only measured since Herschel, 1779).
- **Dumbbell**: a star's puffed-off layers ~1,300 ly away; grey because eyes lose colour in dim
  light; the camera sees it teal (oxygen).
- **Saturn**: rings of ice and rock, a few tens of metres thick; would float in water; Titan bigger
  than Mercury; light ~75 minutes old; rings near edge-on in 2025-26.
- **Andromeda**: a trillion stars, 2.5 million ly, the farthest thing your eyes can see; light
  left before humans existed.
- **Uranus question**: real, a tiny blue-green steady dot; offer a Voyager 2 photo, never an
  AI-made picture.

## Never

- Move a never-homed mount on a guessed counterweight side.
- Two instructions that cross ("take the camera off" and "I'm flashing" in one message).
- Leave the hold off after a STOP or stall while someone's looking: put it back or say so.
