summary: "A night the telescope ran by itself, published as it went: the Triangulum Galaxy, a gold and blue double star, and a list of galaxies and nebulae worked through until dawn."
Location: San Francisco Bay Area. A driveway at home, with trees on most sides
Sky: Clear and calm
Telescope: Celestron 8SE Schmidt-Cassegrain. 203 mm aperture, about 2,085 mm focal length by plate solve, f/10.3
Camera: Sony a6000, stock, at prime focus. APS-C 23.5 x 15.6 mm, 6000 x 4000, 3.9 micron pixels, RAW
Field: 38.8 x 25.9 arcmin in one frame, 0.388 arcsec per pixel
Mount: Sky-Watcher EQ6-R, set down without polar alignment. Aligned from four plate-solved photos and driven from that model, unguided
Computer: Raspberry Pi 3 running Observatory, in Elixir
Frames: 15 second exposures at ISO 3200 for the galaxies and nebulae; much shorter ones for the double star and the small, bright planetary nebulae
Processing: Stacked from the RAW frames with numpy, OpenCV and rawpy. Plate solving by astrometry.net. Nothing generated

I set this night up, picked a list with Claude, and went to bed. The telescope worked through the list on its own. A small AI agent watched over it with a written runbook, and each target's frames were stacked into a picture as soon as the telescope moved on to the next one.

This page went up before the first picture was finished, with the list of what was planned, and it was filled in through the night. A target that says it is being photographed was being photographed when the page was last published.

Earlier in the evening some frames came out with trailed stars. The tracking was letting go of the motors for a few seconds at a time, whenever the network between the telescope and the computer indoors got busy. That was found and fixed before the list started. The Triangulum Galaxy was photographed across the fix, and its page says how many frames it cost.
